# Nine-day revision plan

This plan assumes the exam is on 8 or 9 August 2026 and starts on 30 July. Keep sessions short and stop before fatigue; the aim is confidence and accuracy, not maximum hours.

| Day | Focus | Minimum output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Python variables, types, input/output, arithmetic | 15 code-reading questions |
| 2 | Conditions, comparisons, Boolean logic | 15 questions and 5 small programs |
| 3 | Loops, `range`, strings | Trace 10 loop snippets by hand |
| 4 | Lists, tuples, sets, dictionaries | 10 data-structure questions |
| 5 | Functions and mixed Python code reading | One timed 30-question set |
| 6 | AI basics, ML types, data, training/testing | Explain each concept aloud in one minute |
| 7 | Computer vision, NLP, applications, ethics | Analyse three case studies |
| 8 | Verbal/applied reasoning and full mock | 50 questions in 90 minutes |
| 9 | Review mistakes only; light recap | Prepare device, slot, ID, and sleep |

## Daily routine

1. 10 minutes: recall yesterday's ideas without notes.
2. 25–35 minutes: learn or revise one topic.
3. 20–30 minutes: solve questions.
4. 10 minutes: record mistakes and the corrected rule.

For a Grade 8 student, a parent can help by asking “what rule did you use?” rather than supplying the answer.

## Mock-test rules

- Use a timer and do not pause it.
- Mark uncertain questions and return later.
- After the test, classify every mistake as concept, reading, calculation, or time management.
- Re-attempt missed questions the next day without looking at the solution first.
